# First Test Example

This example shows the shape of a small synthetic usability test. It is illustrative, not real user research.

## Input

**Product:** mobile parking app  
**Fidelity:** low-fidelity wireframe  
**Goal:** explore whether a driver can understand how to start parking  
**Material:** Home → Parking → Vehicle selection → Confirmation

### Provisional persona

**Alex** drives to unfamiliar areas a few times per month, uses mobility apps, values speed and wants to avoid parking fines.

## Wizard setup

**Learning question:** Can Alex understand where and how to start a parking session?

**Free exploration:** Show the Home screen without a mission. Observe what Alex notices, what seems interactive and where parking is expected to live.

**Mental model:** Ask Alex to describe the app and where they would expect vehicle and parking controls.

**Mission:** “You have just parked your car on the street and want to make sure your parking is active.”

Notice that the mission does not say which button, section or label to use.

## Example finding

**Finding:** Alex initially looks for a parking action near the vehicle information before exploring the service menu.

**Category:** Synthetic behavior

**Interpretation:** Vehicle context may compete with service-category navigation as the expected starting point.

**Hypothesis:** Drivers may expect parking actions to be associated directly with the active vehicle.

**Recommendation:** Validate this expectation with real users before changing navigation.

**Limitation:** This is simulated behavior from a synthetic persona and does not establish how frequently real users would behave this way.

## Why this matters

The protocol preserves the distinction between what was provided, what the persona simulated, what the model interpreted and what still requires real validation.
